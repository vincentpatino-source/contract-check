Wyoming's rules for merchant cash advances

Wyoming doesn't have a law written specifically for merchant cash advances, so reading your contract closely matters even more.

  • No MCA-specific disclosure law. As of this update, Wyoming doesn't have a law requiring merchant cash advance funders to give you a standard cost disclosure before you sign.
  • What that means for you: what you're told about the cost, including any APR, depends on what the funder chooses to share. The contract itself is the only full record of your terms.
  • Federal rules don't fill the gap. Federal truth-in-lending rules cover consumer credit, not business financing, so they don't require MCA funders to show you an APR.
  • Confessions of judgment are still allowed. Unlike many states, Wyoming law still provides for judgments by confession, as long as the attorney confessing judgment produces the warrant of attorney to the court and files it with the clerk (W.S. 1-16-201 and 1-16-202). The Wyoming Supreme Court has said a confession of judgment in a promissory note isn't against public policy in Wyoming. If you signed one, our free review flags it and your assigned attorney can review whether it was properly entered.
  • Interest rules are built around consumer credit. Wyoming's interest and usury rules sit in its Uniform Consumer Credit Code. Outside that code, Wyoming's 7% legal rate only applies when there's no agreement for a different rate (W.S. 40-14-106), so business deals generally run on whatever the contract says.

What regulators have done about MCAs

  • FTC vs. Yellowstone Capital. The Federal Trade Commission charged Yellowstone with taking money from businesses' bank accounts without permission. Yellowstone paid more than $9.8 million, and the FTC sent refund checks to 7,731 small businesses.
  • FTC vs. RCG Advances. The FTC alleged RCG Advances deceived and threatened small businesses. A court order permanently banned the company and its owner from the merchant cash advance industry.

Is an MCA a loan in Wyoming?

Wyoming has very little court history on merchant cash advances, and its interest rules focus on consumer credit. In practice, which state's law your contract chooses often matters more than Wyoming law itself.

  • Check the governing-law clause first. Many MCA contracts with Wyoming businesses say New York law applies. New York has a 25% criminal usury cap that corporations and LLCs can raise, and New York courts have treated some MCAs as loans. Our contract check shows you which state's law your contract uses.
  • The same test courts use elsewhere. Courts deciding whether an MCA is really a loan usually ask whether payments truly adjust when sales drop, whether there's a fixed end date, and whether the funder can still collect if the business files for bankruptcy.
  • Formed a Wyoming LLC but operate somewhere else? Many businesses register in Wyoming and run their operations in another state. Where you actually do business, and the law your contract chooses, can matter as much as where you're registered. Your assigned attorney will look at both.
  • How we help. Because Wyoming doesn't require funders to disclose costs, our free check fills the gap: your estimated APR, total payback, any confession of judgment, and whether your payments really adjust with your sales. Your assigned attorney uses that in talks with your funders.

Questions from Wyoming business owners

Are merchant cash advances legal in Wyoming?

Yes. Wyoming has no law written specifically for merchant cash advances, and its interest rules focus on consumer credit, so for business deals the contract terms do most of the work.

Does Wyoming require MCA funders to disclose the cost?

No. As of this update, Wyoming has no law requiring MCA funders to give you a standard cost disclosure. That's why we estimate the APR and total cost from your contract for you.

Are confessions of judgment allowed in Wyoming?

Yes. Wyoming law still provides for judgments by confession, and the attorney confessing judgment must produce and file the warrant of attorney with the court. If you signed one, we'll assign you an attorney licensed in Wyoming to review it and whether it was handled properly.

Is there a usury limit for business funding in Wyoming?

Wyoming's usury rules are built around consumer credit. Its 7% legal rate only applies when there's no agreement on a different rate, so business deals generally follow the contract. If your contract chooses New York law, New York's 25% criminal usury cap may come into play instead.

My business is a Wyoming LLC but I operate in another state. Which rules apply?

Often the state named in your contract's governing-law clause, and sometimes the state where you actually do business. Our free contract check shows you the governing-law clause, and your assigned attorney can explain what applies to you.
