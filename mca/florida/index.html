Florida's rules for merchant cash advances

Florida's Commercial Financing Disclosure Law has required MCA funders to show their costs since January 1, 2024.

  • Cost disclosures before you sign. For deals of $500,000 or less signed on or after January 1, 2024, funders must give you a written disclosure of the cost and payment terms.
  • No APR required. Unlike California and New York, Florida doesn't require funders to show an APR, so you may need to work out the true yearly cost yourself.
  • No registration. Florida doesn't require MCA funders to register with the state. Funders that make five or fewer deals a year are exempt.
  • Brokers can't charge advance fees. The law prohibits brokers from collecting fees from you before you're funded.
  • Who enforces it: the Florida Attorney General. The law doesn't let business owners sue over it directly.
  • Confessions of judgment. Florida law says a power of attorney to confess judgment signed before a lawsuit is "absolutely null and void" (Fla. Stat. § 55.05). A judgment entered in another state can still be brought into Florida, though, so if you signed one, have it reviewed quickly.
  • Check which state's law your contract uses. Many MCA contracts with Florida businesses say New York law applies, not Florida law. Our contract check shows you the governing-law clause, because it decides which usury rules apply.

Updated November 2026. Laws change, so confirm current rules with the state or an attorney.

What regulators have done about MCAs

  • FTC vs. Yellowstone Capital. The Federal Trade Commission charged Yellowstone with taking money from businesses' bank accounts without permission. Yellowstone paid more than $9.8 million, and the FTC sent refund checks to 7,731 small businesses.
  • FTC vs. RCG Advances. The FTC alleged RCG Advances deceived and threatened small businesses. A court order permanently banned the company and its owner from the merchant cash advance industry.

Is an MCA a loan in Florida?

Florida's appellate courts have so far sided with funders on this question, so challenging an MCA as a disguised loan is harder in Florida than in New York. It still comes down to how your specific contract works.

  • Craton Entertainment v. Merchant Capital Group (Third District Court of Appeal, January 2021). The court held that an MCA purchase and sale agreement was not a disguised loan, so Florida's criminal usury statute didn't apply to it.
  • What Florida requires to prove usury. Florida courts look for four things: a loan, an understanding that the money must be paid back, interest above the legal limit, and corrupt intent.
  • The usury limits for true loans. Florida treats interest above 18% a year as usurious, or 25% for loans over $500,000 (Fla. Stat. § 687.02), and interest above 25% a year is criminal usury (Fla. Stat. § 687.071).

Questions from Florida business owners

Are merchant cash advances legal in Florida?

Yes. Florida's Third District Court of Appeal held in 2021 that an MCA purchase agreement was not a disguised loan, and since January 2024 Florida requires cost disclosures on deals of $500,000 or less.

Does Florida require MCA funders to disclose the cost?

Yes, for deals of $500,000 or less signed since January 1, 2024. Florida doesn't require an APR, though, so the disclosure may not show the true yearly cost. Our free check estimates it for you.

What are Florida's usury limits?

For true loans, Florida treats interest above 18% a year as usurious (25% for loans over $500,000), and more than 25% a year is criminal usury. Whether those limits reach an MCA depends on whether a court sees it as a loan, and Florida courts have generally said a true MCA is not.

What about a confession of judgment in Florida?

Florida law says a power of attorney to confess judgment signed before a lawsuit is "absolutely null and void" (Fla. Stat. § 55.05). A judgment entered in another state can still be brought into Florida, though, so if you signed one, we'll assign you an attorney licensed in Florida to review it quickly.

My Florida business signed a contract under New York law. Which rules apply?

Usually the state named in the contract's governing-law clause, though there are exceptions. If it's New York, New York's usury rules and case law may apply instead of Florida's. Our free contract check shows you the clause, and your assigned attorney can explain what it means for you.

I'm behind on my MCA payments in Florida. What should I do?

Reach out early. The earlier you call, the more options there usually are. Call us for a free review of where you stand and what can be done. We'll assign you an attorney licensed in Florida.
