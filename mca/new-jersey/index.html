New Jersey's rules for merchant cash advances

New Jersey doesn't have a law written specifically for merchant cash advances, so reading your contract closely matters even more.

  • No MCA-specific disclosure law. As of this update, New Jersey doesn't have a law requiring merchant cash advance funders to give you a standard cost disclosure before you sign.
  • A bill is pending. New Jersey Senate Bill 1760 would require cost disclosures on merchant cash advances and other business financing of $500,000 or less. As of this update, it hasn't become law.
  • What that means for you: what you're told about the cost, including any APR, depends on what the funder chooses to share. The contract itself is the only full record of your terms.
  • Federal rules don't fill the gap. Federal truth-in-lending rules cover consumer credit, not business financing, so they don't require MCA funders to show you an APR.

Updated November 2026. Laws change, so confirm current rules with the state or an attorney.

Why an MCA can cost so much

A merchant cash advance is written as a purchase of your business's future sales, not a loan. Because it isn't a loan, it generally isn't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans. That is how the cost can run so high even though the contract never calls it interest.
