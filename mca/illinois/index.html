Illinois's rules for merchant cash advances

Illinois doesn't have a law written specifically for merchant cash advances, so reading your contract closely matters even more.

  • No MCA-specific disclosure law. As of this update, Illinois doesn't have a law requiring merchant cash advance funders to give you a standard cost disclosure before you sign.
  • Bills have been introduced. Illinois lawmakers have proposed commercial financing disclosure bills, but none has become law as of this update.
  • What that means for you: what you're told about the cost, including any APR, depends on what the funder chooses to share. The contract itself is the only full record of your terms.
  • Federal rules don't fill the gap. Federal truth-in-lending rules cover consumer credit, not business financing, so they don't require MCA funders to show you an APR.
  • Business loans can carry any agreed rate. The Illinois Interest Act exempts business loans to business associations, partnerships and sole proprietors from its general rate limit (815 ILCS 205/4), so there's generally no usury cap for a business deal in Illinois.
  • Confessions of judgment are allowed in commercial deals. Illinois law lets a confession of judgment be used for a debt that's actually due, and bans it only in consumer transactions (735 ILCS 5/2-1301(c)). If you signed one, our free review flags it and your assigned attorney can act on it.
  • The 36% cap is for consumer loans. You may read that Illinois caps loans at 36% APR. That's the Predatory Loan Prevention Act, which covers consumer loans, not business financing like an MCA.

Updated November 2026. Laws change, so confirm current rules with the state or an attorney.

What regulators have done about MCAs

  • FTC vs. Yellowstone Capital. The Federal Trade Commission charged Yellowstone with taking money from businesses' bank accounts without permission. Yellowstone paid more than $9.8 million, and the FTC sent refund checks to 7,731 small businesses.
  • FTC vs. RCG Advances. The FTC alleged RCG Advances deceived and threatened small businesses. A court order permanently banned the company and its owner from the merchant cash advance industry.

Is an MCA a loan in Illinois?

Because Illinois exempts business loans from its rate limit, winning the argument that an MCA is really a loan doesn't help much under Illinois law. Which state's law your contract chooses usually matters more.

  • Check the governing-law clause. Many MCA contracts with Illinois businesses say New York law applies. New York has a 25% criminal usury cap that corporations and LLCs can raise, and New York courts have treated some MCAs as loans. Our contract check shows you which state's law your contract uses.
  • What Illinois business owners should look for. Check whether your payment really adjusts when your Illinois sales slip, whether the agreement has a set end date, and whether the funder can still collect in bankruptcy. Those three facts decide how a New York court would treat it.
  • Reconciliation still matters. Even without a usury cap, a funder that refuses to adjust payments when your sales fall may not be honoring the deal it wrote. That's often the strongest point in a negotiation.

Questions from Illinois business owners

Are merchant cash advances legal in Illinois?

Yes. Illinois has no law written specifically for merchant cash advances, and its Interest Act lets business lenders charge any agreed rate, so for most business deals the contract terms decide what you owe.

Is there a usury limit for business funding in Illinois?

Generally no. The Illinois Interest Act exempts business loans from its rate limit. The 36% APR cap you may have read about is in the Predatory Loan Prevention Act, which covers consumer loans, not business financing.

Are confessions of judgment allowed in Illinois?

Yes, in commercial deals. Illinois bans them only in consumer transactions. If you signed one, we'll assign you an attorney licensed in Illinois to review it quickly.

Does Illinois require MCA funders to disclose the cost?

No. Illinois has no commercial financing disclosure law as of this update, so nothing requires an Illinois MCA offer to show an APR. Upload your contract and our free check estimates the APR and total payback for you.
