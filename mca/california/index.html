California's rules for merchant cash advances

California was the first state to make MCA funders show business owners what their financing really costs, and it has tightened those rules twice since.

  • Cost disclosures with an estimated APR. Under California's Commercial Financing Disclosure Law, in effect since December 2022, funders must give you a written disclosure on offers of $500,000 or less, including an estimated annual percentage rate (APR).
  • APR whenever a price is quoted (since January 1, 2026). Senate Bill 362 requires the APR to be stated whenever a funder or broker quotes a charge or pricing figure, and bars using the words "rate" or "interest" in a misleading way. In practice, a factor rate like "1.35" can't be presented to you as a rate.
  • Licensing is coming. Assembly Bill 2116, signed September 30, 2026, brings MCA providers and brokers under California's lender licensing law. It takes effect January 1, 2028, and unlicensed providers and brokers can't operate after July 1, 2028.
  • Who oversees it: the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI).

Updated November 2026. Laws change, so confirm current rules with the state or an attorney.

Why an MCA can cost so much

A merchant cash advance is written as a purchase of your business's future sales, not a loan. Because it isn't a loan, it generally isn't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans. That is how the cost can run so high even though the contract never calls it interest.

Questions from California business owners

Are merchant cash advances legal in California?

Yes. Merchant cash advances are legal in California. They're written as purchases of a business's future sales rather than loans, so they generally aren't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans.

Does California require MCA funders to disclose the cost?

Yes. For offers of $500,000 or less, funders must give you a written cost disclosure with an estimated APR before you sign, and since January 2026 they must state the APR whenever they quote you a price.
