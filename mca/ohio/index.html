Ohio's rules for merchant cash advances

Ohio doesn't have a law written specifically for merchant cash advances, so reading your contract closely matters even more.

  • No MCA-specific disclosure law. As of this update, Ohio doesn't have a law requiring merchant cash advance funders to give you a standard cost disclosure before you sign.
  • What that means for you: what you're told about the cost, including any APR, depends on what the funder chooses to share. The contract itself is the only full record of your terms.
  • Federal rules don't fill the gap. Federal truth-in-lending rules cover consumer credit, not business financing, so they don't require MCA funders to show you an APR.
  • Cognovit notes must carry a specific warning. Ohio Revised Code 2323.13 requires a cognovit provision to include statutory warning language displayed more clearly and conspicuously than anything else on the document, and bans cognovit provisions in consumer loans. Ohio courts read these provisions strictly. If you signed one, our free review checks whether the warning meets the statute, and your assigned attorney can act on it.
  • Business loans are exempt from the 8% cap. Ohio's general interest limit (R.C. 1343.01) doesn't apply to business loans to business associations, partnerships and sole proprietors.

Updated November 2026. Laws change, so confirm current rules with the state or an attorney.

What regulators have done about MCAs

  • FTC vs. Yellowstone Capital. The Federal Trade Commission charged Yellowstone with taking money from businesses' bank accounts without permission. Yellowstone paid more than $9.8 million, and the FTC sent refund checks to 7,731 small businesses.
  • FTC vs. RCG Advances. The FTC alleged RCG Advances deceived and threatened small businesses. A court order permanently banned the company and its owner from the merchant cash advance industry.

Is an MCA a loan in Ohio?

Ohio's civil rate limit doesn't reach most business deals, but its criminal usury law is broader, and the question of whether an MCA is really a loan can matter under either Ohio or New York law.

  • Ohio's 25% criminal usury law. Ohio defines criminal usury as charging interest on an extension of credit at more than 25% a year, unless the rate is otherwise authorized by law (R.C. 2905.21(H)). Knowingly engaging in criminal usury is a fourth-degree felony (R.C. 2905.22).
  • Sutton Bank v. Progressive Polymers (Ohio Supreme Court, 2020). The court held that a cognovit note using the statute's warning language was valid, reversing an appeals court that had found it defective. The case shows how closely Ohio courts look at the exact wording of these provisions.
  • Check the governing-law clause. Many MCA contracts with Ohio businesses say New York law applies, where courts have treated some MCAs as loans under a 25% criminal usury cap. Our contract check shows you which state's law your contract uses.

Questions from Ohio business owners

Are merchant cash advances legal in Ohio?

Yes. Ohio has no law written specifically for merchant cash advances. Its 8% interest limit doesn't apply to business loans, but its criminal usury law covers interest above 25% a year when the rate isn't otherwise authorized by law.

What is a cognovit note in Ohio?

It's Ohio's version of a confession of judgment. To be valid, it must include the statutory warning language displayed more clearly and conspicuously than anything else on the document, and it can't be used in a consumer loan. If you signed one, we'll assign you an attorney licensed in Ohio to review it.

What is criminal usury in Ohio?

Ohio defines it as charging more than 25% a year in interest on an extension of credit, unless the rate is otherwise authorized by law. Whether it reaches an MCA depends on whether a court sees the advance as an extension of credit.

Does Ohio require MCA funders to disclose the cost?

No. Ohio hasn't adopted a commercial financing disclosure law, so an Ohio MCA offer doesn't have to state an APR or total cost up front. Our free contract check works those out for you.
