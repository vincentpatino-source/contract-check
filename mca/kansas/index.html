Kansas's rules for merchant cash advances

Kansas has required cost disclosures on merchant cash advances since July 1, 2024, under the Kansas Commercial Financing Disclosure Act.

  • Cost disclosures before you sign. For deals of $500,000 or less, funders must disclose the total funds provided, the amount disbursed to you and the payment terms. Funders that make five or fewer deals in Kansas in a year are exempt.
  • No APR required. Kansas doesn't require funders to show an APR.
  • Brokers can't charge advance fees. Brokers can't collect fees from you upfront, beyond fees for services actually needed to arrange the application, and can't make false or misleading statements.
  • No registration. Kansas doesn't require MCA funders to register.

Updated November 2026. Laws change, so confirm current rules with the state or an attorney.

What regulators have done about MCAs

  • FTC vs. Yellowstone Capital. The Federal Trade Commission charged Yellowstone with taking money from businesses' bank accounts without permission. Yellowstone paid more than $9.8 million, and the FTC sent refund checks to 7,731 small businesses.
  • FTC vs. RCG Advances. The FTC alleged RCG Advances deceived and threatened small businesses. A court order permanently banned the company and its owner from the merchant cash advance industry.

Why an MCA can cost so much

A merchant cash advance is written as a purchase of your business's future sales, not a loan. Because it isn't a loan, it generally isn't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans. That is how the cost can run so high even though the contract never calls it interest.

Questions from Kansas business owners

Are merchant cash advances legal in Kansas?

Yes. Merchant cash advances are legal in Kansas. They're written as purchases of a business's future sales rather than loans, so they generally aren't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans.

Does Kansas require MCA funders to disclose the cost?

Yes. Since July 1, 2024, funders must give you a written disclosure on deals of $500,000 or less. An APR isn't required, so our free check estimates it for you.

I'm behind on my MCA payments in Kansas. What should I do?

Reach out early. Missing payments can trigger default fees and collection activity under many MCA contracts, and options usually narrow the longer it goes. Call us for a free review of where you stand and what can be done.

What about a confession of judgment in Kansas?

A confession of judgment lets a funder get a court judgment without a lawsuit, and whether one can be used against you depends on where it was signed and filed. If you signed one, we'll connect you with an attorney who can review it.
