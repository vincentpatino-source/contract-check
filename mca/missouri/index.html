Missouri's rules for merchant cash advances

Missouri's Commercial Financing Disclosure Law took effect February 28, 2025, and it also regulates the brokers who arrange these deals.

  • Cost disclosures before you sign. For deals of $500,000 or less, funders must give you a written disclosure of the cost and payment terms. Funders that make five or fewer deals a year are exempt.
  • Brokers must register. Brokers must register with the Missouri Division of Finance and carry a $10,000 surety bond. Funders themselves don't register.
  • Written as a purchase, treated as a purchase. When a funder describes the deal as a purchase of receivables, Missouri law treats that as conclusive that it isn't a loan.
  • No APR required. Missouri doesn't require funders to show an APR.

Updated November 2026. Laws change, so confirm current rules with the state or an attorney.

What regulators have done about MCAs

  • FTC vs. Yellowstone Capital. The Federal Trade Commission charged Yellowstone with taking money from businesses' bank accounts without permission. Yellowstone paid more than $9.8 million, and the FTC sent refund checks to 7,731 small businesses.
  • FTC vs. RCG Advances. The FTC alleged RCG Advances deceived and threatened small businesses. A court order permanently banned the company and its owner from the merchant cash advance industry.

Why an MCA can cost so much

A merchant cash advance is written as a purchase of your business's future sales, not a loan. Because it isn't a loan, it generally isn't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans. That is how the cost can run so high even though the contract never calls it interest.

Yes. Merchant cash advances are legal in Missouri. They're written as purchases of a business's future sales rather than loans, so they generally aren't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans.
