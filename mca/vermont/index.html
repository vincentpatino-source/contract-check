Vermont's rules for merchant cash advances

Vermont passed one of the strictest MCA laws in the country in 2026. It takes effect July 1, 2027.

  • Today: Vermont doesn't yet have rules specific to merchant cash advances. What you're told about the cost depends on what the funder chooses to share.
  • Starting July 1, 2027: Act 142 (H.648), signed June 16, 2026, will require MCA funders to hold a Vermont license and brokers to hold a solicitation license.
  • Full disclosures, including an estimated APR. Before you sign, funders will have to disclose the finance charge, estimated APR, total repayment, payment terms, fees, collateral and any refinancing details. It covers deals under $1 million.
  • Limits on automatic bank debits. Funders will only be able to set up automatic debits if they hold a first-priority security interest.
  • Confessions of judgment. Starting July 1, 2027, any commercial financing contract with a confession of judgment provision will be void in Vermont.

Updated November 2026. Laws change, so confirm current rules with the state or an attorney.

What regulators have done about MCAs

  • FTC vs. Yellowstone Capital. The Federal Trade Commission charged Yellowstone with taking money from businesses' bank accounts without permission. Yellowstone paid more than $9.8 million, and the FTC sent refund checks to 7,731 small businesses.
  • FTC vs. RCG Advances. The FTC alleged RCG Advances deceived and threatened small businesses. A court order permanently banned the company and its owner from the merchant cash advance industry.

Why an MCA can cost so much

A merchant cash advance is written as a purchase of your business's future sales, not a loan. Because it isn't a loan, it generally isn't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans. That is how the cost can run so high even though the contract never calls it interest.

Questions from Vermont business owners

Are merchant cash advances legal in Vermont?

Yes. Merchant cash advances are legal in Vermont. They're written as purchases of a business's future sales rather than loans, so they generally aren't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans.

Does Vermont require MCA funders to disclose the cost?

Not yet. Starting July 1, 2027, funders will have to be licensed and give you a full written disclosure, including an estimated APR. Until then, it's up to the funder, so it's worth having your contract checked.

Reach out early. Missing payments can trigger default fees and collection activity under many MCA contracts, and options usually narrow the longer it goes. Call us for a free review of where you stand and what can be done. We'll assign you an attorney licensed in Vermont.

What about a confession of judgment in Vermont?
