New York's rules for merchant cash advances

New York's Commercial Finance Disclosure Law covers merchant cash advances up to $2.5 million, one of the highest limits in the country.

  • Cost disclosures with an estimated APR. Since August 1, 2023, funders must give you a written disclosure before you sign, including an estimated APR, the total you'll repay, your payment amounts and any prepayment terms.
  • Covers deals up to $2.5 million. Funders that make five or fewer deals in New York in a year are exempt.
  • Renewals must be disclosed. If part of a new advance goes to paying off unpaid charges on an earlier one, the funder has to tell you. This is often called "double dipping."
  • Who oversees it: the New York State Department of Financial Services (DFS).

Updated November 2026. Laws change, so confirm current rules with the state or an attorney.

Why an MCA can cost so much

A merchant cash advance is written as a purchase of your business's future sales, not a loan. Because it isn't a loan, it generally isn't subject to the interest-rate limits that apply to loans. That is how the cost can run so high even though the contract never calls it interest.
