Bankruptcy restrictions in MCA contracts

Many merchant cash advance contracts include terms that try to stop you from filing bankruptcy, or punish you if you do. These clauses can make owners feel trapped. In general, courts don't let a contract take away your right to file.

What these clauses look like

  • A promise that the business won't file bankruptcy while the advance is outstanding.
  • Filing bankruptcy listed as a breach or default, with default fees or the full balance coming due.
  • The funder keeping the right to collect from the business or the owner personally if the business files.

Can a contract stop you from filing?

Generally, no. Courts have long held that promising in advance not to file bankruptcy is unenforceable, because public policy strongly favors access to bankruptcy relief. The right to file is treated as fundamental, and a total ban on filing has been called contrary to both the Constitution and public policy.

That doesn't mean bankruptcy is always the right move. But a clause in your MCA shouldn't be the reason you think it's off the table.

Why it matters beyond bankruptcy

When courts decide whether an MCA is really a loan, one of the three main questions is whether the funder can still collect if the business files for bankruptcy. A contract that protects the funder from that risk looks more like a loan, which can open the door to usury defenses. See Is your MCA a loan in disguise?
