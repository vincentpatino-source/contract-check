Juggling multiple MCAs? What to do when you're stacked

Taking a second or third advance to cover the first is how most MCA trouble starts. Each new funder adds its own daily or weekly debit, and before long the payments can take more than your business brings in.

How stacking happens

A broker calls with a fast approval. The new money helps for a few weeks, but now there are two debits instead of one. Then a third offer arrives. Some brokers promise a cheaper loan later if you keep paying (a tactic known as carroting), and that loan rarely comes.

Signs it's become a problem

  • A large share of your deposits goes straight to funders
  • You're taking new advances to make payroll or rent
  • You're offered renewals before the last advance is paid off
  • You can't keep any cash cushion in the account

Anti-stacking clauses, and why funders often ignore them

Most MCA contracts say you can't take another advance. Yet funders routinely fund businesses whose bank statements clearly show existing advances. A funder that knew about your other advances when it funded may have a weaker complaint about stacking later. That's one of the things worth looking at when your contracts are reviewed.

Why another advance usually isn't the fix

You can't borrow your way out of debt, especially at costs that would be considered usury if these deals were treated as loans. Some funders will still push it.

A common version is the renewal: a funder you already have offers you more money and tells you to use it to pay off your current balance. What actually happens is that a new factor rate gets charged on the balance you already owed. You end up paying interest on interest, and the total you owe grows even though it feels like a fresh start. That's one of the main ways owners get stuck in the cycle, and most don't get out until they talk to a restructuring expert.

When even the funders won't give you more money, the next offer is usually a reverse consolidation. It's sold as relief, but it's one more advance with its own fees and debits.

Dealing with all your funders at once

The most effective approach is to restructure every advance together, based on what your business can actually afford:

  • One plan that covers every funder, so none of them jumps ahead
  • An attorney licensed in your state assigned to your case
  • Payments typically 50 to 80% lower than what you're paying now, with complete legal protection
  • Fees built into the savings. On average, our clients pay less in total than what their funders claim they owe
