Where an MCA funder can sue you

Most MCA contracts include a clause choosing New York law and New York courts, so many business owners get sued far from home. But that clause isn't the end of the story.

Rules that can block or move a case

  • Unregistered funders (BCL § 1312(a)): a company from another state that does business in New York without authority can't maintain a lawsuit in New York until it's authorized.
  • Out-of-state against out-of-state (BCL § 1314): New York limits when one non-New York company can sue another non-New York company in its courts. New York judges have dismissed MCA cases on this ground.
  • Changing venue: New York law allows a case to be moved to a different county in certain situations.

Why it matters

Being sued in another state makes it harder and more expensive to defend yourself, and confessions of judgment have historically been filed far from the business. Jurisdiction and venue defenses can sometimes end a case or move it somewhere fairer.
