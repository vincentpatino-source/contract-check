Receiverships and merchant cash advances

A receiver is a neutral person a court appoints to take control of a company's assets and operations. In the MCA world, receiverships usually come up when a funder itself is shut down by regulators.

Example: Par Funding

In July 2020, at the request of the SEC, a federal judge in Florida appointed a receiver over Complete Business Solutions Group, which did business as Par Funding, one of the larger MCA companies. Par Funding later pleaded guilty to defrauding the investors who financed its merchant cash advances.

What it means if your funder is in receivership

  • Your advance usually doesn't disappear. The receiver typically steps in to collect it.
  • A receiver may be more open to a reasonable settlement than the original funder was.
  • Court orders in the receivership case can affect how and where disputes are handled.
