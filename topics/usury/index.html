Usury and merchant cash advances

Usury laws cap how much interest a lender can charge. MCAs are written as sales so they fall outside those caps, but when a court decides an MCA is really a loan, usury can become a powerful defense.

New York's two usury limits

  • Civil usury, 16% a year: the general limit on loans. Businesses set up as corporations or LLCs generally can't use this one.
  • Criminal usury, 25% a year: charging more than this is a crime under New York law. New York's highest court held in 2021 that corporate borrowers can raise criminal usury as a defense in a lawsuit.

These usury limits don't apply to loans of $2.5 million or more.

Why this matters for MCAs

Many MCAs work out to yearly rates far above 25% when you calculate them. If a court finds the MCA is really a loan, and the rate is above the criminal limit, New York's highest court has said a criminally usurious loan is void from the start. That can change what, if anything, you still owe.

Whether an MCA is a loan depends on how the deal works, which is explained in Is your MCA a loan in disguise?

Other states

Usury rules vary widely. Some states, like Georgia, Missouri and Louisiana, have laws saying deals written as purchases of receivables aren't loans or that the charges aren't interest. See MCA rules by state.
