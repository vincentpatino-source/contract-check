MCA reconciliation: the catch behind lower payments

Reconciliation sounds like a safety valve: if your sales drop, your payment drops too. But when a funder offers to reconcile, be careful. Funders often add a default fee automatically once you start paying the lower amount, so the relief you asked for ends up raising what you owe.

How reconciliation is supposed to work

Because an MCA is written as a purchase of a percentage of your sales, many contracts include a reconciliation clause. You send recent bank statements, the funder compares what it collected with the agreed percentage of your actual sales, and your payment is adjusted.

The catch: reconciliation as a path to default fees

Funders often treat a reconciled payment as a missed payment and add a default fee automatically. Watch for terms like these:

  • A lower payment counts as a breach. If you pay the reconciled amount before the funder formally approves it, or the funder later disputes it, the shortfall can be treated as a default.
  • Strict paperwork and deadlines. Missing a document or a deadline during the review can end the reconciliation and trigger default fees.
  • The funder decides alone. Some clauses let the funder accept or deny a request at its sole discretion, then treat any reduced payment as a missed one.
  • One-way adjustments. Some contracts let the funder raise payments after a review, or end your reconciliation right after a single missed payment.

Once a default is declared, many MCA contracts add a default fee, make the full balance due at once, and open the door to a lawsuit. The relief you asked for can end up costing you more.

Before you ask for reconciliation

  • Read exactly what your contract requires, and whether a reduced payment or a missed step counts as a default.
  • Get any approval in writing before paying a lower amount.
  • Keep copies of every statement and message you send.
  • Talk to someone who knows MCAs first, especially if you have more than one advance.

Why courts care about this

When courts decide whether an MCA is really a loan, they look closely at whether reconciliation actually works. A right that's hard to use, or that the funder can deny or turn into a default, points toward a disguised loan. See Is your MCA a loan in disguise?
