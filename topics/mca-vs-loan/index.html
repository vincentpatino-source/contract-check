MCA vs. business loan: what's the difference?

Brokers often use the words interchangeably, and on your bank statement the daily debits look the same. But a merchant cash advance and a business loan are built differently, and those differences decide how much you pay and what your options are.

The key differences

  • What it is. A loan is borrowed money you repay with interest. An MCA is written as a sale of your future sales: the funder buys them at a discount.
  • How the cost is shown. Loans show an interest rate and, in most cases, an APR. MCAs use a factor rate, which hides how expensive they are.
  • How you pay. Loans usually have monthly payments. MCAs usually take daily or weekly debits straight from your account.
  • Rules that apply. Loans are covered by lending laws, including state limits on interest. Because MCAs are not written as loans, funders argue those limits don't apply to them.
  • Paying early. Paying off a loan early usually saves interest. With most MCAs the full cost is fixed on day one.

When an MCA is really a loan

Calling a deal an MCA doesn't settle it. Courts look at how it actually works: Is there a real reconciliation right if your sales drop? Is there a fixed end date? Does the funder really lose if your business fails? When the answers point to a loan, a court may treat it as one, and then usury laws can come into play. See Is your MCA a loan in disguise?

Loan or MCA, the way out is similar

Plenty of business owners carry both: an MCA or two alongside a high-cost online loan. It doesn't matter which is which. Both can be restructured, and the goal is the same: lower payments and protection for your business.
