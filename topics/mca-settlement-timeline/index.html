How long does MCA settlement take?

There's no single answer. Some files are resolved in a few weeks, while others take several months, especially when there are several funders or a lawsuit. Here's what happens along the way and what affects the timeline.

The usual stages

  • Review. Your contracts, bank statements, payment history and any notices or court papers are reviewed to see what you really owe and what leverage you have.
  • Strategy and outreach. A plan is built around what your business can actually afford, and each funder is contacted.
  • Negotiation. Funders respond at different speeds. Some agree quickly; others push back before they settle.
  • Written agreements. Every deal is put in writing, including releasing the funder's UCC filing once the agreement is complete.
  • New payments. You pay under the new terms instead of the old daily or weekly debits.

What speeds it up

  • Experienced MCA attorneys ready to respond immediately, so funders know you're protected from day one
  • Reaching out early, before a lawsuit or judgment
  • Having your contracts and recent bank statements ready
  • A realistic proposal based on your real cash flow
  • Funders that settle regularly, which many do

What slows it down

  • Several funders, each with its own process
  • Disagreements about how much is actually owed
  • Lawsuits or judgments that are already in place
  • A balance sold to a debt buyer, which adds a new party to the negotiation

Lump sum or lower payments

Settlements usually take one of two forms: a single discounted payoff, or a structured plan with smaller payments over time. Most owners don't have a large lump sum available, so the goal is often a lower, manageable payment. Our clients' payments are typically 50 to 80% lower than what they were paying, with complete legal protection, and fees are built into the savings.

While it's in progress

  • Don't take another advance to cover payments. It adds a new funder and makes the process longer.
  • Send funder calls, emails and letters to your team instead of negotiating on your own.
  • Keep running your business and keep good records of your deposits.
