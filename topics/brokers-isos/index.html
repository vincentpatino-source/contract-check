MCA brokers (ISOs): how they're paid

Most merchant cash advances are sold through brokers, often called ISOs (independent sales organizations). The broker's commission is usually built into the cost of your advance, even if you never see a separate bill for it.

What to know

  • Commissions raise your cost. The broker's pay typically comes out of the deal, which means a higher payback or a smaller amount reaching you.
  • Brokers earn more on renewals. A new advance that pays off an old one often earns the broker another commission.
  • Some states require disclosure. Utah requires funders to disclose any part of the funding paid to a broker.

States that regulate MCA brokers

  • Registration: Texas, Virginia, Connecticut and Missouri require MCA brokers to register, and Vermont will require a license starting July 1, 2027.
  • No upfront fees: Florida and Kansas prohibit brokers from collecting advance fees from you.
