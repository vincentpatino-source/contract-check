Predatory and unfair MCA terms

Regulators and courts have called out a long list of MCA practices as predatory. Knowing what to look for helps you see whether your contract or your funder crossed a line.

Speak with a restructuring expert Check my contract free Debt calculator

Terms and practices that raise red flags

  • Fixed daily debits that don't change with your sales, despite a promised reconciliation.
  • Less money reaching you than promised because of hidden fees.
  • Withdrawals taken without permission or after the balance was paid.
  • Stacking: funding a business that already has several advances, sometimes in breach of the funder's own contract.
  • Threats or harassment during collection.

Legal theories owners use

  • Usury: the MCA was really a loan above the legal rate limit. See Usury and MCAs.
  • Unconscionability: terms so one-sided and unfairly obtained that a court won't enforce them.
  • Mutual mistake and misrepresentation: the deal didn't match what both sides understood or what you were told.

These are fact-specific, and courts apply them carefully. An attorney can tell you which, if any, fit your situation.
